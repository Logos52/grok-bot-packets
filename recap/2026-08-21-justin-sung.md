**Open / two meanings of faster**

- --:-- · Justin · Two readings of "faster": coverage speed (how much material in a certain time) vs time to outcome (total time to the exam, the upskill, the new project).
- --:-- · Justin · Most people reject a method that takes twice as long to cover the same volume.
- --:-- · Justin · Covering is the very very very first out of a hundred steps; most of the time that matters is rehearsal — relearning, gaps, relations, testing.

**Doctor on specialist exams**

- --:-- · Justin · 10 hours on this, retention pretty good, like 80%, mastery really high, "just so slow."

**Method 1 — increase frequency of prediction errors**
- --:-- · Justin · Asks chat, out of 10, how much "increase your frequency of prediction errors" already makes sense; gets a range.
- --:-- · Justin · A prediction error is a signal that you need to learn it in a different, better, more accurate way; it drives deeper understanding and strengthens memory.
- --:-- · Justin · It occurs when you, number one, make a prediction, and number two, realize you were wrong. That's it.
- --:-- · Justin · Scan keywords, headings, learning objectives; pause; predict the points; then read. Most predictions will be wrong.
- --:-- · Justin · Game-show / million dollars: even with foreign terms, dissect the word — first part "demi," second part looks French, related to another word.
- --:-- · Justin · Do not randomly guess; try your best; that draws existing connections and becomes the schema the brain builds on when the error arrives.
- --:-- · Justin · Any level: before the first paragraph, before a video, 2 hours into a concept, one technical paragraph.
- --:-- · Justin · Habit: predict, be wrong, next round more detailed and nuanced; as frequently as possible.

**Method 2 — multi-pass encoding**
- --:-- · Justin · Do not try to learn or understand everything the first time; only spend attention on what feels easy enough.
- --:-- · Justin · Prior knowledge is one of the most influential predictors of how quickly and easily you learn and how long you retain.
- --:-- · Justin · Baseball study: students split by baseball knowledge and by reading ability; knowledge of baseball beat high reading ability with no baseball knowledge.
- --:-- · Justin · Prior knowledge is in your control, not a CV meme of 35 years and seven degrees; you choose to build it.
- --:-- · Justin · Skip what is way out; first pass expands knowledge; on the second or third pass the hard parts become as easy as the first.
- --:-- · Justin · Staircase: every step is the same distance from the last; do not jump to the second story in one go.
- --:-- · Justin · First pass: 80 90% you skim; then an additional 10 or 20% becomes relevant; you do not need seven or eight passes.
- --:-- · Justin · Two or three shorter passes can be as fast or faster than the linear slog — reread the paragraph, still do not remember.
- --:-- · Justin · Each pass carries tiny prediction errors.
- --:-- · Justin · About half an hour in; these two methods are enough; 70% of his toolkit; plane, car, before sleep, no iPad or paper.
- --:-- · Justin · Hundreds of other videos exist; if you take nothing else, focus on these two first.

**Q&A — whole textbook**
- --:-- · guest · How would you approach an entire textbook? I go too deep into every topic, waste time on the details, then later forget them.
- --:-- · Justin · Least important: consuming. Most important: what you do before and after.
- --:-- · Justin · Do not ambush the brain; if you throw a ball with no warning they will not catch it.
- --:-- · Justin · Working memory lasts roughly 15 seconds and holds roughly four items; it cannot support three pages and 20 minutes of reading.
- --:-- · Justin · Read then make sense later: by the second paragraph the first is already being lost.
- --:-- · Justin · A week later: memory of writing the notes, none of the material — like someone else's notes.
- --:-- · Justin · Prime with prediction errors before the pages; that frames what is relevant, cuts unnecessary depth, and deepens retention.

**Q&A — what to stop**
- --:-- · guest · Some of my learning habits may be slowing me down. What should I stop if I want to learn faster?
- --:-- · Justin · Two answers. First: the habit that blocks the transformation — measuring covering-speed, holding the old method tightly. You cannot go from slow to fast overnight; unfit to marathon.
- --:-- · Justin · Effective learning first, then efficient; people will not commit to the new method because they still score speed, not time to outcome.
- --:-- · Justin · Second: writing notes for the sake of writing notes. School myth: you are not learning if you are not writing; writing is learning.
- --:-- · Justin · Write-vs-read studies: better attention an hour later. That is not weeks or months, and not applying concepts.
- --:-- · Justin · Write fewer notes, fewer words; more time thinking; more selective about what you write.
- --:-- · Justin · Litmus: of a week's study time, how much is notes; after those notes, what exam score would you get.

**Q&A — lecture notes in real time**
- --:-- · guest · In a fast content-heavy lecture, what does higher-order note-taking look like when you cannot pause to synthesize or compare?
- --:-- · Justin · Learn comparing and synthesizing on written text first, where you can pause; then apply in real time.
- --:-- · Justin · Not every sentence is conceptually dense; tune out; use that as the pause; jot a few keywords.
- --:-- · Justin · Recommendation still: start with written text.

**Q&A — systems that die in week one**
- --:-- · guest · I design good learning systems, then abandon them within a week when course load intensifies. How do I make a system sustainable?
- --:-- · Justin · No better test than reality: stress, time pressure, tired, sleep deprived, three, four books at once.
- --:-- · Justin · Prediction errors is a method, not a system. A system is when you use it, what is before and after, how it feeds notes and the schedule.
- --:-- · Justin · Multiple tools, multiple points of failure — that is good; if reality fails it, that is the truth, even if the theory looks ideal.
- --:-- · Justin · Do not throw the baby out with the bathwater; the system is a template; tiny modifications; never done. That is the hallmark.

**Q&A — cognitive load / confusion compass**
- --:-- · guest · How do I increase my cognitive load tolerance?
- --:-- · Justin · Amount of mental effort you are willing to tolerate. He often uses the term; he thinks he might have invented it.
- --:-- · Justin · Short answer: you kind of can't. What you get better at is managing the load so you do not need to tolerate as much.
- --:-- · Justin · Beginner: consume, stay confused, give up or brute-force memorize.
- --:-- · Justin · Intermediate: predictions, analogies, teaching, retrieval, mind mapping — then overwhelmed by connections, angles, tests, new gaps.
- --:-- · Justin · Overwhelm means working memory is nearing capacity; stay there and the brain is busy holding, not connecting.
- --:-- · Justin · Detect that feeling and change strategy so load comes down to an active but manageable band.
- --:-- · Justin · If there was a third strategy: the confusion compass. Feel confused → "What is the question that if I had the answer to, would make me feel less confused?"
- --:-- · Justin · Loop: take information, get confused, targeted questions, less confused, more information.
- --:-- · Justin · Asks chat what one moment was worth the time if they learned nothing else.

**Q&A — transfer, default mode, reflection**
- --:-- · Sylvester · Methods seem to primarily focus on academics. Do they translate to unrelated domains?
- --:-- · Justin · They do not primarily focus on academics; most powerful usage is work or life; science of how the brain learns any new information it must remember and use.
- --:-- · Justin · This is puerh tea, not coffee; a liter of coffee and he would be bouncing off the walls.
- --:-- · guest · Why can some people concentrate on sports but not on academic or cognitive tasks?
- --:-- · Justin · Default mode network: at rest the brain is globally active; zoning out is that network kicking in.
- --:-- · Justin · The activity's own active focus keeps you off default mode; brain gets bored if it is not doing something fun.
- --:-- · Justin · Washing dishes, wiping a floor, a non-stimulating walk: zone out. Sports with teamwork and observation: locked in.
- --:-- · Justin · Passive reading slips to default mode. Fight it with predictions, analogies, teaching, micro retrieval, simplify, map, compare to prior knowledge.
- --:-- · Justin · Do the thing which creates the concentration. It is the other way around.
- --:-- · guest · What is the most effective method for reflecting so you can improve a skill quickly?
- --:-- · Justin · You cannot reflect your way to success. Reflection is not automatically beneficial. You need practice data, then iterate by acting out a prediction and getting feedback.
- --:-- · Justin · Ball: every kick veers left; predict foot and ankle; angle the foot right; still wrong — that is a prediction error.
- --:-- · Justin · "This is bad, this is bad" with no prior prediction: the brain does not know where to put the feedback.
- --:-- · Justin · Abstraction: the underlying tendency, habit, trend that repeats in other domains — that is usually the most bang for buck; that becomes the next prediction.

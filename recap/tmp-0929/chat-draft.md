# All-In: Daniel Ek

- Spotify’s origin was a Sweden-local bet against piracy: founders put their own prior-startup cash on the table to guarantee labels’ next-year bonuses, then scaled UK → US.
- Neko Health is that same playbook in healthcare — Sweden lab, UK proof, now US — selling a four-hundred-ninety-nine-dollar vertically integrated hour meant to catch disease early instead of paying for late acute care.
- Ek’s core claim is incentive failure: US care still spends nearly a fifth of GDP on a system built for infectious disease, while employment-tied insurance tenures kill decade-scale preventative ROI.
- The product bet is multimodal longitudinal data (fifty-three blood markers, thousands of skin images indexing hundreds of moles, wearables via Apple Health) plus same-hour clinicians — over a hundred thousand scans and about one percent serious undiagnosed finds so far, with positive unit economics.
- On AI he refuses a strong pace-the-frontier vote, says the industry under-sells upside (music personalization, mole tracking), wants open and closed models to coexist, and floats compute volume as the under-discussed guardrail.
- Other pins quiet this pass: Moon Shawn Ryan, Justin Sung clarity essay, Maxinomics Airbnb, Fern influencer campaign, Frontpage gig economy, and no newer first-party Elon sit-downs.

### One paragraph
On the 25th anniversary, Konstantin and Francis walk investigative journalist Richard Miniter through cub-reporter basics of 9/11 for listeners who never lived it. Miniter's arc runs from the day itself — four hijackings, ~3,000 dead, Flight 93 passengers discarding "cooperate" orthodoxy to spare ground civilians — back to 1979 (Iran, Mecca, Soviet Afghanistan) as the forge for Azzam-style fighting jihad and a bin Laden who turned a fighter base/database into a network. The close lands on ordinary-faced heroism (Rumsfeld's burned hands, firefighters re-entering, Miniter's nearly-lost WSJ brother) and prevention via campus isolation mechanics — because knowing the West does not guarantee loving it.

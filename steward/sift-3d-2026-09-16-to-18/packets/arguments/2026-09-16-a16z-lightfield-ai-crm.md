### One paragraph
Joe Schmidt and Alex Rampell walk Lightfield CEO Keith Peiris through an atypical AI founder arc: Tome hit ChatGPT-era escape velocity, then the team killed it because they didn’t love the decks and couldn’t see a path past missing human context — “hopium” that the next GPT bump would fix storytelling. Following heat from sales pilots, they discovered the real pain was reconciling conflicting customer reality across CRM, calls, and warehouses, rebuilt an AI-native CRM as a chronological “business world model,” and used absurd greenfield tactics (office space as negative price) until a barely-finished product earned hourly Slack rage — the opposite of Tome’s polite disengagement. The product bet is intelligence over schema: activity logs first, semi-structured inference, schema-less sync, expansion answers that read people and product usage; the GTM bet is win new companies, give the whole org free seats so a Salesforce-trained VP can’t rip it out, and brownfield-wedge on understanding/steering rather than “AI sends the email.” Pricing settled only after seat-skew and credit-freeze failures into platform+seat for core capture plus consumption for pipeline/automations/forecasting — and culture is the other product: no swim lanes, generalists, continuous re-rank, ship bar via bug bashes — because in a red-ocean CRM race the nightmare is being the slow dashboard vendor startups abandon for Salesforce.

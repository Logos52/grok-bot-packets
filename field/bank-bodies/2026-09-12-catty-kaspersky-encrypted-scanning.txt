Hey, thanks for the detailed report. Here’s the key point up front: nothing needs to be re-provisioned on our side. Your account is entitled, your Agent Computer is up and healthy, and entitlement and privacy checks for your account are responding today without errors from Cursor and from cursor.com . So there’s no need to use Reset or Recover, they won’t help here. What we’re seeing: the Grok Bot app on your PC connected normally during the day on September 10, and after that not a single request from the app reached our servers. The ConnectError.Internal entries are what the app logs when the secure connection is getting cut off on the PC itself, before the request is even sent. Most often, the cause is something on the PC or network inspecting HTTPS traffic using its own certificate. Cursor and your browser trust the Windows system certificate store, so they work. The Grok Bot app currently trusts only public certificate authorities, so it fails. This also explains why your direct HTTPS tests to the Agent Computer hosts succeed. Try these in order: In Edge or Chrome, open https://api2.cursor.sh , click the lock icon, then Connection is secure, then the certificate icon, and check the Issued by field. It should say Amazon Amazon RSA 2048 M01. If it shows something else (an antivirus, a security product, or something from your university or organization), that’s the cause. Check whether any security software is scanning HTTPS traffic. It’s usually called Encrypted connections scanning, SSL/TLS filtering, HTTPS scanning, or Web protection in products like Kaspersky, ESET, Avast or AVG, Bitdefender, Norton, Trend Micro, Sophos, and similar. If it’s enabled, turn it off or add Grok Bot to exclusions. Then fully quit Grok Bot from the system tray and relaunch it. If nothing changes, connect your PC to your phone hotspot (turn off Wi-Fi), then fully quit Grok Bot again, relaunch it, and wait a couple of minutes. Then tell us what you saw in step 1, whether step 2 or 3 helped it connect, and the name of the security software on the PC. If it still doesn’t work, attach this file: %APPDATA%\Grok Bot\desktop-structured-log-spill.v1.json . One more thing: the app downloaded 0.47.0 on September 10, but you’re still on 0.44.0. Once you’re connected again, install the latest version from Cursor · Download Grok Bot .

--- @deanrie [CursorStaff] | 2026-09-11T09:54:08.153Z ---
Great, glad it’s working, and thanks for coming back with the details, that really helps. So this confirms it: Kaspersky with Encrypted connections scanning enabled was swapping the certificate issuer to Kaspersky Anti-Virus Personal Root Certificate instead of Amazon. Because of that, Grok Bot couldn’t establish a secure connection. As soon as scanning was turned off and the issuer was Amazon RSA 2048 M01 again, everything started working. A couple notes for next time: If you turn Encrypted connections scanning back on in Kaspersky, the issue will come back. In that case, you don’t have to disable scanning entirely, you can add Grok Bot to the exceptions list. Once you’re reliably online, update the app to the latest version from Cursor · Download Grok Bot . You were on 0.44.0 and there’s a newer one available.

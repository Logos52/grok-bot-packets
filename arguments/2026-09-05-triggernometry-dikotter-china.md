### Arguments
- 01:13 · Dikötter · **Claim:** The China we know geopolitically is still an undeconstructed Qing empire. **Support:** 1949 inherits Qing borders from the Manchu conquest (1644–1911); Xinjiang camps, Tibet, one time zone / Mandarin / "organic" Chinese nationality driven from Beijing. **Rejects:** treating modern China as a post-imperial nation-state that decolonized like other empires.
- 01:13 · Dikötter · **Claim:** Nineteenth-century China was extraordinarily bloody, and Westerners still undercount and romanticize it. **Support:** Taiping-era conflict ~70M+ dead; with other rebellions, death toll well over 100M; Western books often cite ~20M with no footnote trail; Voltaire-style civilization myth persists. **Rejects:** China-as-Confucian-wisdom vs Europe-as-savagery, and the idea Chinese brutality is uniquely "Chinese" rather than human / Mongol-method conquest.
- 01:13 · Dikötter · **Claim:** The CCP did not rise as a mass popular force from land reform. **Support:** Comintern founding (Hank Sneevliet / Moscow); ~1 in 12,500 by mid-1930s vs far higher communist density in Salazar's Portugal or banned Finland; no Russian-style serfs/aristocratic estates — small plots, little land to redistribute; early base areas "squeezed like a lemon" (Zhou Enlai, 1934). **Rejects:** textbook Nationalists-vs-Communists as the decisive social-force narrative, and Edgar Snow / Red Star Over China as history rather than fountainhead propaganda.
- 24:07 · Dikötter · **Claim:** Mao's distinctive skill is power, not agrarian idealism. **Support:** holds back while Nationalists fight Japan, then occupies vacated terrain; alliances and pledges broken as needed; "politics in command" from Great Leap through COVID slogans ("politics first, science second"). **Rejects:** Mao as poor peasant / true-believing egalitarian first; vision yields to all-consuming defense of monopoly power (Xi's military-commission purges as parallel).
- 24:07 · Dikötter · **Claim:** Soviet occupation plus American policy, not social revolution, delivered 1949. **Support:** ~1M Soviet troops strip and hand Manchuria countryside to CCP; Wallace / Marshall push coalition and ceasefire; May 1946 threat to cut Nationalist arms when CCP is near extinction; Soviet ship/wagon weapons and training. **Rejects:** "agrarian reformers / margarine communists" American framing, and asking cultural-philosophical "why China went red" the way one wrongly would for East Germany or Poland.
- 24:07 · Dikötter · **Claim:** Communist civil-war method is attrition without regard for civilian life. **Support:** barrier troops (zagraditelnye); unarmed human waves; Changchun eight-month siege (~160,000 civilian dead); other cities surrender knowing that fate (Beijing). **Rejects:** parity between Nationalist and Communist willingness to use the gun.
- 53:07 · Dikötter · **Claim:** The Great Leap is a political leap to overtake the USSR / lead the socialist camp, executed by turning the countryside into foot soldiers — and famine is engineered via food-as-weapon. **Support:** full collectivization by ~1956–57; dormitories, work points, canteen cutoffs for pregnant/sick/old/"undesirables"; sparrow eradication → locusts; backyard furnaces melting tools for steel quotas; Mao informed and doubles down after 1959 criticism; ≥45M dead 1958–62. **Rejects:** "Mao didn't know" / intermediaries-only guilt, and framing GLF as mere economic miscalculation.
- 53:07 · Dikötter · **Claim:** The Cultural Revolution is loyalty theater to pre-empt de-Maoification. **Support:** 1966–68 people denounce party; army purges people; after Lin Biao's 1971 death the army is purged; loyalty proved by denouncing intimates; Liu Shaoqi tortured to death. **Rejects:** CR as primarily about culture or sincere egalitarian renewal.
- 01:11:22 · Dikötter · **Claim:** Contemporary China has not abandoned communist command of the economy; shiny GDP is a quota illusion. **Support:** state owns land, ~99.5% bank assets, energy, big industry; billionaires can vanish; bullet-train debt (~$900B cited) / empty trains paid by non-consuming households ("temple rich, monks poor"); dig/fill/build/destroy all book as GDP; ghost cities and statistical lies (Gogol dead-souls analogy); export surplus from domestic inability to buy. **Rejects:** shopping-mall / Gucci tourism as proof China left communism, and Western applause for trade-surplus "success" without reading misallocation.
- 01:11:22 · Dikötter · **Claim:** You cannot "compete" with China by copying its model without becoming unfree. **Support:** local subsidy races, below-cost energy/land, WTO outlet for unsold mountains of goods; Hayek road-to-serfdom — ban unions, nationalize, crush speech, zero labor rights, keep people poor to subsidize dumps. **Rejects:** "just compete better on the same terms" as a liberal strategy.

### One paragraph
Konstantin and Francis ask Frank Dikötter how communist China was made and why outsiders keep mistaking its glitter for success. Dikötter's through-line: the PRC inherits an undeconstructed Qing empire; the CCP was a tiny Comintern plant whose rise was Soviet occupation of Manchuria plus American coalition/embargo folly, not land-reform destiny — sealed by ruthless attrition (Changchun's 160,000 starved). Mao is cast as a student of power ("politics in command") who drives the Great Leap (≥45M dead; food as weapon; sparrows and backyard furnaces) and a Cultural Revolution of loyalty purges to protect legacy. On today's China he rejects Gucci-mall "post-communism": state command of land, banks, and industry turns GDP into a Stalinist quota — empty trains and towers paid by poor monks while the temple looks rich — with export overcapacity the outlet for goods locals cannot buy. He closes that competing by copying that model is a road to serfdom, and that the remedy is studying Chinese history rather than tourist optics.

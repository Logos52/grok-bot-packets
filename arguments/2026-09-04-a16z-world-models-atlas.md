### Arguments
- 02:55 · Justin · **Claim:** Atlas’s fundamental primitive is new view prediction, not next-frame video. **Support:** inputs (views or a scene description) form a spatial context; a virtual camera at an arbitrary point in space and time gets the predicted appearance. **Rejects:** treating Atlas as just another video / next-frame model among the “bajillion” claiming novel views.
- 03:51 · Ben · **Claim:** Pose-grounded spatial context is what separates Atlas from omni-reference video models. **Support:** every image carries a 3D camera pose, so four corner views of a room reproduce exact geometry rather than text-prompt “slot machine” retries. **Rejects:** image-only conditioning and high-level text control as enough for precise reconstruction or directed fly-throughs.
- 05:15 · Justin (+ Fei-Fei) · **Claim:** Unifying generation and reconstruction in one model is a new architecture, not scaled video. **Support:** reconstruction was its own CV subfield; generation was diffusion/text-to-video; Atlas is multimodal from the start (text, image, video, camera poses, depth maps) at pre-training. **Rejects:** “obvious scaled-up traditional video model.”
- 08:50 · Fei-Fei · **Claim:** Atlas is a significant step on the path to spatial intelligence. **Support:** spatial intelligence must generate space, reason in it, and edit/interact (ultimately 4D); camera pose per frame is the critical geometric signal; “pixels that are truly spatially contextualized and grounded” vs mere pixel generation. **Rejects:** equating gazillions of pixel generators with spatial intelligence.
- 16:08 · Ben · **Claim:** Sparse capture (~3 views / 50–100× fewer photos) flips who can reconstruct and what footage is usable. **Support:** dense recon is medical-imaging-like exhaustiveness (100–300 photos / hours for casuals); Atlas can reuse internet/casual/old clips and throw away 95% of old dense captures. **Rejects:** productizing 3D only via exhaustive dense capture.
- 17:30 · Justin · **Claim:** Generation and reconstruction must interplay; triangulation alone always leaves holes. **Support:** classic recon needs multi-view triangulation; unseen pixels are holes unless imagined; even Ben with a DSLR misses under tables/mics; Stanford-quad demo: ground inputs → aerial fly-through “according to the laws of reconstruction.” **Rejects:** pure dense triangulation or pure unconstrained generation as sufficient.
- 21:27 · Fei-Fei / Justin · **Claim:** Scaling + next-viewpoint prediction were the right bets; architecture/data mixture details were the risk. **Support:** early-summer smaller model flew under the NeRF garden table with the soccer ball — “decision within 5 seconds”; bigger/longer/more chips kept improving; release size limited by deadline, not data. **Rejects:** being at the end of this architecture’s scaling ladder — “we’re at the beginning,” compute-bound.
- 24:42 · Ben · **Claim:** Creatives need stateful, 3D-consistent worlds and editable control, not ephemeral text-prompt generations. **Support:** Marble users often only wanted alternate viewpoints from a splat; image models rearrange rooms across views; mapping director feedback into 3D is ~95% of fabrication/architecture work. **Rejects:** one monolithic video model and throwaway generations as how spatial creative work actually runs.
- 29:22 · Fei-Fei · **Claim:** Atlas unblocks robotics real-to-sim; robotics’ bottleneck now is data (later chips). **Support:** acquired Synnex (real↔sim); prior dense recon was laborious and slowed velocity; need randomization of cable bends, box sizes/colors, scene layout; Atlas as next-gen sparse recon for that pipeline. **Rejects:** treating robotics as just another artifact-generation problem solvable from web images/code alone.
- 32:47 · Justin · **Claim:** A learned world simulator can become the planner; understanding action→world response is the world-model thesis. **Support:** robotics policies are agents facing unexpected world responses, so training needs coverage of failure modes; classical physics engines vs data-driven neural sims; imagining the action that produces a desired response is the same core. **Rejects:** stopping at static world generation or classical sim alone.
- 35:56 · Justin · **Claim:** Dynamics are not at odds with good 3D; exposing dynamics improves even static outputs. **Support:** Marble architecture/training were fundamentally static; Atlas architecture and data support dynamics (“baby dynamics”: water waves, moving cars); pre-train saw dynamics, this release’s post-train emphasized static/spatial; latent dynamics remain. **Rejects:** needing fully static data to get frozen-time recon, and the critique that Atlas has no dynamics.
- 41:58 · Justin (+ Fei-Fei) · **Claim:** Generative new view prediction is AI-complete, the spatial analogue of next-token prediction. **Support:** Ilya-style mystery-novel “and the killer was…”; movie frames then predict who walks out; Fei-Fei: evolution gave eyes to movers, not trees, because motion yields new viewpoints. **Rejects:** next-token as the only AI-complete primitive worth building toward for physical intelligence.

### One paragraph
Martin Casado sat World Labs’ Fei-Fei Li, Justin Johnson, and Ben Mildenhall the day after Atlas launched to pin down what a “world model” is beyond video-model marketing. Justin’s frame: Atlas does generate, reconstruct, and simulate, but the base primitive is new view prediction — spatial context in, virtual camera anywhere in space and time out — with camera poses and depth as native modalities, unifying generation and reconstruction that CV kept in separate tracks. Ben’s sparse-vs-dense case is commercial as much as technical: dense recon’s hundreds of photos become ~3 / 50–100× fewer, so casual, internet, and old footage become reconstructible, while generative fill-in closes the holes triangulation always leaves (Stanford quad from ground shots). They claim scaling + new-view conviction paid off early (NeRF-table under-fly Slack; first pre-train cycle “insane”; still compute-bound, not architecture-dead). Use cases split creatives (persistent 3D-consistent viewpoints vs slot-machine video; architecture’s 3D-revision grind) and robotics (Synnex real-to-sim, data/randomization bottleneck, neural sim that could itself plan). On the “more dynamics” critique: Marble was static; Atlas already has baby dynamics and was pretrained on motion even when this checkpoint’s post-train favored static. Close: Justin and Fei-Fei cast generative new-view prediction as AI-complete and the evolutionary twin of next-token prediction — eyes for animals that move.

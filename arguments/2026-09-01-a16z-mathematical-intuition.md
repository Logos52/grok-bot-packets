- 06:12 · Litt · **Claim:** Models are strong at applying known techniques and weak at intuition / theory-building. **Support:** Claude and ChatGPT look similar on the same small set of problems; ChatGPT got useful for research math earlier, Claude caught up around Opus 4.5/4.6; both fail autonomous theory-building, succeed with hints. **Rejects:** "math is a verifiable domain" as the full explanation of progress, and the claim that solving named problems equals doing mathematics.
- 20:26 · Litt · **Claim:** The results that look creative (unit distance) are counterexamples that import techniques; proving true conjectures that need new machinery is still out of reach. **Support:** community thought unit distance was true, then a construction refuted it; his own multi-year conjectures sit in a framework that needs new ideas, not a clever counterexample. **Rejects:** counting the models out forever — he is not a skeptic of continued capability growth; he guesses a different RL environment might do it.
- 33:33 · Litt · **Claim:** The goal of mathematics is understanding, not papers, and current incentives will burn the pipeline. **Support:** Codex experiment — "find five recent conjectures in algebraic geometry and prove them" yielded three correct-but-bad papers in an hour; three to five identical proofs of the same theorem posted within days; postdocs can play the slot machine. **Rejects:** treating understanding that lives only in model weights as success, and subordinating exploration to what the model wants to pursue (one mathematician duplicated a thousand times).
- 43:26 · Litt (Lisha pushing) · **Claim:** Even if models become robustly superhuman, humans should not hand over math research. **Support:** "optimal" unsupervised exploration might take a direct path instead of diverse curiosity; easiest guarantee of broad fundamental research is a community with broad interests pushing the models. **Rejects:** Lisha's adjacent fear that labs' mode-collapsed proofs plus missing human intuition could stall superior results — Litt goes further: optimality does not mean you should do it. ### One paragraph
Lisha asked what AI can actually do in research math versus the headlines. Litt's case: the mid-May Erdős unit-distance solution is his favorite fully autonomous result because it was a bit creative — a counterexample that imported 1960s techniques — but frontier models still do a narrow band of human-looking technique-application, not intuition, philosophy, or autonomous theory-building. He rejects both "math progress is just verifiable RL" and "the proofs are alien." They aligned that understanding, not paper-count, is the point: his Codex run produced three correct-but-bad papers in an hour, identical proofs are landing on arXiv days apart, and short clever writeups exist because long ones cannot be checked (800-page singularities claim; models miss structural errors). Lisha feared mode collapse plus missing human intuition could stall the field without people in the loop; Litt went further — even superhuman unsupervised research might take a direct path instead of diverse curiosity, so keep a community with broad interests. Split: he is not a capabilities skeptic (different RL might unlock theory-building). Close: use the tools to think better, not to stop thinking.

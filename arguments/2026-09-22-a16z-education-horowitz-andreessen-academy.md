### Arguments
- 01:05 · Ben Horowitz · **Claim:** College is a mismatched product for 18-year-olds whose stated goal is a job or a company — large financial/time spend mostly optimizes for scholar-track bundling, not building. **Support:** ask why they go → “get a job”; most college activity isn’t around that; skill needs in tech evolve too fast for a big system to keep up; firm still thinks college is great for *most* kids. **Rejects:** treating degree-track as the default right answer for every builder-oriented teen.
- 01:05 · Ben Horowitz · **Claim:** The AI revolution is as transformational to jobs as the industrial revolution was to agricultural society — so industrial-revolution training will not map perfectly, and a pioneer path is needed. **Support:** universities exploded to train corporate literacy after farms→companies; America built on university backs; now jobs/kinds of jobs change under AI; more contained instruction + individual AI-paced tool time + structured social time beat all-day lectures. **Rejects:** assuming legacy lecture-heavy curricula are sufficient for the world teens will live in.
- 01:05 · Gagan Biyani · **Claim:** Prior alt-college experiments failed because they were too narrow and didn’t ship the competitive *bundle* (learning + status/credential/brand + fun + employer connection + residential social dynamics). **Support:** college is a dozens-of-things bundle (sports, hedge funds, etc.); need indication “you went here → I hire/work with you”; only ~2–3 serious attempts at the full bundle in ~50 years; classic startup lesson = serve a specific burning customer — elite young builders who will employ thousands / raise large rounds. **Rejects:** learning-only or other half-bundle hybrids as truly competitive with college’s value prop.
- 01:05 · Erik Torenberg / Ben · **Claim:** a16z + Gagan is the scarce combo — portfolio access (~1,600 companies) plus a CEO who already lived the idea — which earlier education bets lacked. **Support:** search made Gagan obvious (Udemy, Maven; decade+ entrepreneur); Ben: he had the idea longer and worked it without the firm’s resource; firm has backed space experiments that didn’t cut through. **Rejects:** another narrow training product without hiring graph + brand.
- 15:26 · Ben Horowitz · **Claim:** Being 18 now is historically one of the best times — teens can out-capability 30–40-year veterans with new tools — but only if they stay future-forward rather than clinging to fading past ladders. **Support:** Edison/Ford analogy; nervousness comes from broken school→work systems, not lack of problems; can attack problems directly as leader or teammate; immigrant great-grandfather sweatshop / sewing-machine sleep → CCNY grandfather → better father generation; predicts today’s youth will see parents as “sleeping under sewing machines” relative to what’s coming. **Rejects:** doom about youth prospects as the dominant frame; slow 20-year status ladders as the only path.
- 15:26 · Ben Horowitz · **Claim:** Easy-to-build ≠ skip experience — Zuckerberg-scale compulsion is rare; most should join people who’ve done it (companies or classmates) the way SGI shaped Ben. **Support:** Zuck roommates who declined Facebook regretted it; Ben had no great idea at 18; Silicon Graphics innovation as career foundation; dating/pizza-app cold starts as the kid trap vs Databricks-style immersion. **Rejects:** “tools are easy so just found immediately” as general advice.
- 23:18 · Ben / Gagan · **Claim:** California / SF must host it because that’s where company-starting knowledge, culture, and >80% of a16z-connected employers concentrate — SF *is* the campus. **Support:** America one of two giant company-creation poles; Ben’s NY college → finance pipeline contrast; SF embraces failure (fund next company), positive-sum pay-it-forward, high trust + ambition; future of AI/money/biotech argued and built here. **Rejects:** classroom-walls-only campus; locating away from the employer/osmosis graph.
- 26:44 · Ben Horowitz · **Claim:** Academy must be a separate company (connected brand, not a firm subunit) so ambition isn’t capped at “tech investing desk” and can expand to music/medicine/etc. as every profession changes. **Support:** firm’s unique asset = comprehensive tech opportunity graph + explicit grad partnerships; academy ≠ investment product; limiting to firm org would limit ambition. **Rejects:** running it as an internal a16z program forever.
- 28:45 · Ben / Gagan · **Claim:** For-profit with student-as-customer is required so incentives don’t fracture across alumni/donors/parents — and the goal is to be copied, not to monopolize. **Support:** everything built to train youth was for a soon-gone world; “please Harvard, copy us”; profit motive → deliver for students whose success creates brand flywheel; unique education incentive alignment. **Rejects:** multi-customer nonprofit capture that produces disjointed product; monopoly framing.
- 31:01 · Erik / Ben / Gagan · **Claim:** Shift education from push/cram to curiosity-pull with mandatory *output* — AI makes memorization questions obsolete; Boneh’s bar is problems so hard you need AI. **Support:** “lighting a fire” quote; Jobs calligraphy; Ben’s lifelong rap → authenticity/storytelling; Boneh: ban AI won’t work vs hard problems students solve that he couldn’t; main-character vs NPC; calligraphy test vs write an Islamic poem for the academy; horticulture/kitesurf still as projects. **Rejects:** grade-cramming as skill acquisition; banning AI in coursework as serious strategy.
- 35:15 · Ben / Gagan · **Claim:** People skills (and CEO inner game) are learned by doing under social pressure, not from books/lectures — academy orients around putting teams together with practice-level stakes. **Support:** millennial loneliness/screens; AI may free humans from screens to talk to people; Nebraska-roommate style curiosity; robot-dog / cold-email forced learning beside experts; football-book vs 300-lb rusher; hesitation/unresolved conflict costs you only learn by feeling them. **Rejects:** negotiation-lecture pedagogy as sufficient for founder people-skills.
- 37:29 · Ben / Gagan · **Claim:** Constraint is choosing what’s worth working on — don’t settle for a million-dollar idea when you lack a billion-dollar one; explore, fail for earned secrets, then attack related hard problems. **Support:** diseases, environment, election integrity as example problem space; entrepreneurial discovery often accidental (pancreatic cancer path → adjacent discovery); SV values learning-rich failure that earns a secret, not all failure; academy exposes teens beyond teenage life range. **Rejects:** premature small-idea lock-in; romanticizing failure without learning.

### Closing
Ben Horowitz, Erik Torenberg, and Gagan Biyani use the launch of Horowitz Andreessen Academy to argue that AI-era work needs a new education product: residential San Francisco, employer-connected, project-and-people-skills heavy, for-profit with the student as customer, and explicitly *not* a replacement for college for most kids. Ben’s through-line is historical — universities scaled for industrial literacy; AI is a comparable break — so lecture-cram pipelines and multi-stakeholder nonprofit capture won’t train builders who must use tools, ship output, and learn conflict under pressure. Gagan adds the startup diagnosis that prior alt-schools under-bundled (credential, fun, residential osmosis, elite demand), while a16z’s portfolio graph plus a CEO who already wanted this for a decade is the scarce stack. The closing pedagogy bets on curiosity-pull, Boneh-hard AI-required problems, failure that earns secrets, and a five-year scoreboard of loved careers and expanded creative possibility — with an open invitation for Harvard and everyone else to copy.

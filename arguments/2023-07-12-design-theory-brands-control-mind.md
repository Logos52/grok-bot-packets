### One paragraph
John Mauriello maps branding and design onto a Saffir–Simpson-style “BS Continuum,” arguing that the same tools that once solved information asymmetry (marks on goods, costly trust signals, emotional shortcuts) scale into reciprocity traps, fake social proof, aura-of-authority ads (“More doctors smoke Camels”), artificial scarcity FOMO, and Category-5 unity cults (Nike×Kaepernick as tribe banner, ~$6B upside cited) where brands cosplay as spiritual community. Along the way he grants placebo and visual shorthand their due — stripes, packaging, even fake vents can change perception and sometimes performance — then draws the ethical bright line at educate vs manipulate: influence for the other’s benefit with consent versus for yours without. Meta-honest “cult” brands (Liquid Death, dbrand) can sit surprisingly low on the Continuum when claims track reality, because branding’s real job is creating meaning (the autographed dollar), not lying about specs; the structural villain is the company-as-profit-machine that collapses noble boardroom goals into “what’s best for the company” under ordinary fear. Closing move is self-implicating satire merch that weaponizes every Continuum trick with a wink — audience in on the joke, not conned into thinking it’s real magic.

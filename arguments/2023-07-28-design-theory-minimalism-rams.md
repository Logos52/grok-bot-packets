### One paragraph
John Mauriello takes Dieter Rams’ Ten Principles off the pedestal — with Rams’ own blessing that they were never meant to be permanent — and stress-tests each against contemporary practice: “honest” design is often intentional enchantment and salesmanship (acetate tortoise, Braun calculator feelings, render theater); “as little design as possible” has ossified into exclusive, gesture-heavy, gray monoculture that Apple-izes Bauhaus accessibility into luxury; environmental “less but better” is eco-efficiency Band-Aids versus cradle-to-cradle eco-effectiveness; thoroughness must leave room for chance; not every product should be an unobtrusive tool; understandable/useful/innovative/aesthetic are context-bound. The constructive update keeps Rams’ real superpower — systems thinking that tries to shape egalitarian humane living — and lands on Liz Engel’s line that good design bows to life’s fundamentals with Mother Earth as the ultimate user, plus Mauriello’s own sketch: good design communicates what is necessary.

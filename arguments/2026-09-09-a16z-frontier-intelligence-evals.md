- 01:24 · Ryan (Vals) · **Claim:** Building generation systems and building evaluation mechanisms are tightly coupled — a new legible eval is itself a major driver of model capability. **Support:** research background building benches; early 2024 many non-OpenAI models shipping while it got harder than ever to ascertain what was newly capable; first-principles need for a third party solely for high-quality evals; first benches 2024, now industry-wide realization. **Rejects:** treating eval as a lagging scorekeeper detachable from the capability flywheel.
- 02:37 · Ryan · **Claim:** Labs’ internal benches drive progress, but self-reported public-benchmark capability is not trustworthy market evidence — private held-out higher-signal benches expose the gap. **Support:** Llama 4 “disaster”: underperformed on Vals held-out private benches while open public Q+rubric benches showed incredible capability; labs want a rational buying market and Demis/others call for third-party evaluator ecosystems; trillion-dollar industries historically need independent testing. **Rejects:** labs as sole credible “benchmarking stores,” and equating open-leaderboard scores with real capability / investment justification.
- 08:56 · Ryan · **Claim:** Eval firms must refuse selling training data to labs or they recreate auditor–consultant conflicts (Enron / pay-to-pass). **Support:** early Vals decision never to sell training data despite lab pressure; data vendors now use gimmick-style benches as GTM; same group auditing and consulting → pay to win the benchmark; market does not benefit. **Rejects:** lucrative data-side revenue as compatible with independent scoring integrity.
- 11:08 · Ryan · **Claim:** RSI potential needs a shared apples-to-apples index built from process proxies, not only end-to-end self-training runs. **Support:** ideal world = frontier model trains next version and measure delta — too expensive/slow; proxies across pre-training, post-training, harness engineering; score where models do novel research vs struggle; labs already talk RSI in cards without shared language. **Rejects:** waiting for full closed-loop self-training as the only measurable RSI signal, and lab-private RSI claims without cross-model comparison.
- 12:11 · Ryan · **Claim:** Deprecating saturated benches is necessary — eval builders must perpetually construct the next mountain, and benches must reflect the current state of the world. **Support:** unofficial motto “always a higher peak”; as labs hill-climb, Vals’ job is next peaks; economy analogy (agriculture → new labor forms); lawyer/doctor recertification / updating legal-research benches when case law moves. **Rejects:** cherry-picking popular saturated benches for marketing ranks, and static forever-tests divorced from current law/medicine/practice.
- 16:28 · Ryan · **Claim:** Independent evals are existential for enterprises, not only labs — intelligence is misvalued at every stack layer as token spend approaches (or exceeds) salary. **Support:** Fortune 10 Claude Code $100→$300/employee/day anecdote (rate-limit walks; 4–6pm productivity); engineers don’t know how to apportion usage; Anthropic narrow margins to serve; token line-item will demand sharper ROI; long-run “a firm really is just its eval.” **Rejects:** treating enterprise eval as optional nice-to-have vs lab PR, and arbitrary per-seat token budgets as a substitute for legible ROI evals.
- 19:17 · Ryan · **Claim:** In-house enterprise eval expertise is valuable but insufficient alone amid exploding model/harness/agent options — third-party products (private-repo coding benches) remedy compounding complexity. **Support:** more labs, more models/hyperparams/harnesses/agents, growing use-case complexity; hard to build internal eval capacity alone; codegen highest enterprise spend → product that builds internal coding bench from GitHub for performance + ROI; best OpenAI vs Anthropic for *your* repo is non-intuitive until you run it; Sonnet sometimes costlier than Opus (token hunger). **Rejects:** “enterprises know their tasks best, so only in-house” as a complete strategy, and public-repo / list-price intuition as enough to pick models.
- 27:16 / 30:06 · Ryan / Ben · **Claim:** Right policy division: government sets and enforces what it fears (bio/cyber etc.); competent private evaluators measure capability and elicitability so innovation isn’t slowed by gov doing the testing. **Support:** two countervailing forces — don’t slow tech vs align to public interest; evidence-gathering informs what “aligned” looks like, third parties build test/enforce tech that keeps up with frontier; Ben: labs warn gov; gov specifies unwanted behaviors; “can the model do it” + “can you get the model to do it”; gov ill-suited to the latter over time but good at rules; labs’ “we’ll keep the capable model internal” doesn’t always work. **Rejects:** gov as primary ongoing eval operator, purely abstract policy without empirical grounding, and lab self-policing of dual-use capability as sufficient.

### One paragraph
In a short bookshelf tour, John Mauriello answers the recurring “what should I read?” question with six working texts: Norman’s *Design of Everyday Things* as the annotated primer he still cites; *Designing for Growth* and *Value Proposition Design* as the bridge from craft to business-literate design thinking; Gail Greet Hannah’s *Elements of Design* as the Rowena Reed Kostellow formal-language lineage behind his silhouette/sub-shape teaching; *Manufacturing Processes for Design Professionals* as the thick reality check so engineers don’t silently own design decisions (“end very poorly”); and Smithsonian’s *Design: The Definitive Visual History* as a broad movements primer. He soft-launches his own forthcoming course, notes affiliate links, and closes on humble tools — Schneider ballpoint, laptop, paper — insisting sensibility comes from books plus making, not software mystique.

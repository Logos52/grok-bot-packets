### One paragraph
John Mauriello treats Ferrari’s first EV (captioned “Luché”) — designed under Jony Ive’s utility-first rhetoric and sold out (~500 units) despite mass hate — as the visible tip of a decades-long flattening: when feeling and meaning are stripped from objects that once hunted desire, phones, furniture, cafes, logos, and even faces converge on the same sterile median. He grants that Apple’s blank precision fits an iPhone that must disappear as a portal, then argues the same language is a category error on a Ferrari whose object *is* the content, and that Ive is less villain than heir to Loos’s moral minimalism, his own iPhone template, and a feed algorithm that rewards a cycle of bland copies and shock memes (Cybertruck), now turbocharged by thoughtless AI. Downstream, VC signal-gaming (Taste Labs’ $18M “AI against AI slop”) and a Silicon Valley immortality/bunker ethos replace Carnegie-style civic legacy, so the future stops being built for shared humanity — which is why the Luché feels “passive-aggressive” and why “nobody believes in the future anymore.” The constructive half points at cracks that hand power back (Cyberpunk concept cars as imaginative permission, Solar Punk’s attempt, Barcelona superblocks, Mari’s DIY manuals, Framework, Daylight), then leans on Damasio: emotion is how we decide, so design-as-spreadsheet science is a category mistake. Closing prescription: stop begging for a seat at an executive table that rewards the thing designers hate — flip it, bet with your gut like Fuller/Colani, and build humanistic, emotional work in public with others.
